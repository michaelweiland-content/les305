Ethical Theories and Personal Boundaries

Use ethical theories to apply the language and logic of major philosophers and schools of thought. Then define personal boundaries before incentives, fear, loyalty, or time pressure can weaken them.

A credo is a boundary document, not a slogan. It states what you will and will not do when professional rewards, relationships, or pressure are at stake.

The Trolley Problem

A runaway trolley is heading toward five workers on the main track. You can pull a lever to divert it to a side track where one worker stands. The visual previews the two paths; it does not resolve the ethical question.

Preview the Two Paths

Choose a preview to see which track the trolley follows.

Analyze before deciding. Use ethical theories to decide what to do: pull the lever or not? Remember, if you do nothing, five people will perish. If you pull the lever, one person perishes. Engage in ethical analysis that does not begin with "I think," but instead with "According to this ethical theory or philosopher..." Draw on major moral philosophers and ethical schools of thought to apply their language and logic.

Personal Credo Builder

Your writing is private to this activity and is not evaluated or sent to Canvas. Define your ethical boundaries before an ethical dilemma arises, while you have time to think. Preparing a credo before an ethical dilemma arises is key because we tend to make ethical mistakes under pressure when we are unprepared. Ask yourself: What are my ethical boundaries? What would I never do to get a job, get a promotion, or win a contract? A scientist might say, "I would never falsify my research data to get published." A chief financial officer might say, "I would never sign off on financial records without knowing they are true and accurate." A professor might say, "I would never grade a student's work without reading it."

What values should your conduct consistently express?

What would you never do to get a job, promotion, contract, grade, or other advantage?

What will you do if a leader, client, peer, or deadline pushes you toward a boundary?

Who or what will help you pause, test the decision, and speak up?

Edit the draft until it sounds like a commitment you could use in a real decision.
